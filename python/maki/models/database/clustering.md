# Clustered Database

The shape of the colour array relates to the dataset:

  1. It's length is determined by the number of unique $k$-mers in the dataset.
  2. It's width is determined by the number of unique $k$-mer distributions throughout the dataset.
  3. It also takes space to store the distributions.

Assume a star phylogeny between $N$ genomes, i.e. they all derive from a common ancestral sequence. Consider only mutation by substitution, not indels or rearrangements. Assume that each pair of genomes is related by a pairwise mutation rate $r$, equivalent to a branch length $q=r/2$. Let $M=L-k+1$ represent the number of $k$-mers in a sequence of length $L$.

The probability that a $k$-mer is unchanged from the ancestral sequence is

$$
a=(1-r/2)^k.
$$

Assume $k$ is large enough that any mutation generates a unique $k$-mer. Then, for a specific position in the ancestral sequence, the number of new $k$-mers generated by mutations within that region is $N(1-a)$. The probability the original sequence survives in at least one sequence is $1-(1-a)^N$. Therefore, the total number of unique $k$-mers is

$$
\begin{equation}
E\left[U_{total}\right] \approx M\left\{ 1-(1-a)^N + N(1-a) \right\}.
\end{equation}
$$

The expected number of $k$-mers conserved within a specific subset of $s$ genomes among the full cohort of $N$ is given by $M\cdot a^s(1-a)^{N-s}$. We can therefore approximate the number of subsets of genomes within the cohort that are expected to contain at least one $k$-mer as

$$
\begin{equation}
E\left[ C_{total} \right] \approx \sum_{s=1}^{N}{N\choose s}\mathbb{1}\left[ M\cdot a^s(1-a)^{N-s} > 1 \right].
\end{equation}
$$

With these approximations, the size of the colour array is proportional to

$$
E\left[U_{total}\right] \cdot \log_2 E\left[ C_{total} \right].
$$
